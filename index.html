SmolVLA pick-and-place on SO-ARM101

Fine-tuned the SmolVLA vision-language-action policy to place two objects on a plate with a two-camera SO-ARM101. I collected the demonstrations by teleoperation, trained on a laptop GPU, then ran a fixed evaluation protocol on the real arm instead of stopping at “it worked once”.

60ktraining steps (~21 epochs), up from an epoch-starved first run
79teleoperated episodes, 57,519 frames
  1. Dataset audit first. The first runs were epoch-starved (≈1.4 epochs). Re-trained for 60k steps (~21 epochs).
  2. Scored every failure. 11 of 18 failures were gripper placement: a missed grasp or the jaw landing on the object.
  3. Found a timing cause. Generating a 50-step chunk took ~1.9 s with the arm frozen, then it played the chunk for another ~1.9 s without looking again. It saw the scene only once every ~3.8 s, so the control loop ran at 13 Hz instead of 26 Hz. This explains carrying an empty gripper to the plate: the grasp missed, but the arm didn’t see it until the next look.
  4. Checked the noise. Re-sampled the policy 32× per decision point offline. Apart from the first decision, samples stayed tightly grouped, so the failures weren’t sampling luck.

DECEPTACON: a robot that rolls up like an armadillo

A team project: a sphere that unfolds into a four-legged walker. My part was the mechanical design and fabrication: the CAD, every 3D print, print-quality and tolerance tests, and the bearing layout. My teammates built the electronics.

  1. V1 leg: Servobot-style with an extra retract joint. Too bulky: the motor had to travel out with the leg.
  2. V2 leg: the leg is a shell panel, and one servo swings it out. That's 2 servos per leg instead of 3.
  3. Failure under gait loads: the printed rods carrying the bearings snapped. I swapped them for metal bolts and spacers, but the bearings still popped out.
  4. Lesson: printed rods can't carry bearing loads. Seat the bearing in a housing that clamps it, and test the leg under walking loads before printing the whole body.

STS3215 servo driver, from the bus protocol up

The SO-ARM101 runs on Feetech STS3215 serial-bus servos. Instead of calling the vendor SDK, I wrote the driver myself: packet framing, checksums, register map and unit conversions. The core library is shared between a Windows serial backend and the Arduino IDE.

  1. Portable core: plain C++11 compiled by both g++ and the Arduino toolchain. Platform I/O sits behind one backend.
  2. Testable offline: a simulated bus checks packets with no hardware attached.
  3. On hardware: ping, position read and write on a real servo. An Uno demo drives it from a 16×2 LCD menu, buttons and a pot.
  4. Next: error-path handling, a measured multi-servo read rate, and joystick-driven IK running on the Uno itself.
  • C++11
  • UART half-duplex
  • Arduino
  • Win32 serial

NeuroPAL: neuroevolution for StarCraft macromanagement

Combines NEAT with Punctuated Anytime Learning. A fast, loose evaluation steers evolution, and periodic accurate evaluations correct it through fitness biasing. I implemented and evaluated the macromanagement agent, and presented the paper at the IEEE Conference on Games in Lisbon (Rearden travel fellowship).

O’Connor, J., Lee, Y., & Parker, G. B. (2025). NeuroPAL: Punctuated Anytime Learning with Neuroevolution for Macromanagement in Starcraft: Brood War. IEEE CoG, 1–7.

Neuroevolution of compact search heuristics in Sokoban

Can evolution find a tiny learned heuristic that A* can use on unseen puzzles? I built a forward-BFS trajectory generator with deadlock pruning to get exact cost-to-go labels, then trained NEAT and CoDeepNEAT heuristics and compared them with Manhattan distance and a DeepCubeA-style CNN.

BagelEngine: a Vulkan engine from scratch

A 3D engine I started writing in November 2023 and keep coming back to. It started with batch rendering and an ECS and grew into PBR, physics and procedural worlds. I document each step in a 30-entry devlog on the GitHub wiki.

  1. Renderer: bindless descriptors, indirect and instanced drawing, secondary command buffers, PBR with normal maps, cascaded shadows, bloom, SMAA.
  2. Data-oriented core: EnTT components laid out contiguously for cache-friendly iteration. Jolt physics.
  3. Content: glTF loading with skeletal animation, YAML scene files, a procedural planet generator with stacked noise layers.
  4. Tooling: an in-engine console, plus a remote console with an MCP server so an agent can drive the engine and take screenshots. The shots on this page were captured that way.
